CS401 Glossary

Plain-English definitions paired with the exact exam terms used across every CS401 lesson.

Term Definition Lesson
Bit The smallest unit of information in a computer: a single switch that is either OFF (0) or ON (1). Lesson 01
Byte A group of 8 bits side by side. One byte can store a number from 0 to 255 (or a single text character like 'A'). Each memory locker holds 1 byte. Lesson 01
CPU (Processor) The "chef" / brain of the computer: fetches instructions from memory, understands them, and does math and logic using its internal registers. Lesson 01
Memory (RAM) The "pantry": a huge wall of numbered lockers, each holding 1 byte. Holds both your program's instructions and its data. Memory is passive ("dumb")—it only obeys the CPU. Lesson 01
Register The "cutting board": a tiny, ultra-fast storage slot built directly inside the CPU so it can hold and work on numbers without walking back to RAM every step. Lesson 01
Address Bus A one-way (unidirectional) bundle of wires from CPU to RAM that carries the locker number (where to read or write). On the Intel 8088, it has 20 wires (20 bits), allowing 1 MB of lockers. Lesson 01
Data Bus A two-way (bidirectional) bundle of wires that carries the actual byte (what is being moved) between CPU and RAM. On the Intel 8088, it is 8 bits wide (1 byte at a time). Lesson 01
Control Bus Wires carrying command and timing signals (like READ or WRITE) telling RAM what action to perform and when. Lesson 01
iAPX88 (Intel 8088) The microprocessor taught in the first half of VU CS401 (used in the original IBM PC). It has a 16-bit internal design, 14 registers, a 20-bit address bus (1 MB RAM), and an 8-bit data bus. Lesson 01
Assembly Language A human-readable shorthand for the CPU's raw 0s and 1s (machine code), using short words like MOV and ADD. Lesson 01
